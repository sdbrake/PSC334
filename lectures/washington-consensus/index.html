The Washington Consensus

The Washington Consensus refers to a serious of policies that all of the American policymakers and economists suddenly started to believe were the keys to growth:

The Washington Consensus

Fiscal and Monetary Discipline
Public spending is only for growth or welfare
Tax systems should involve everyone and have marginal rates

The Washington Consensus

Interests rates and prices should be liberalized
Trade should be free
Investment should be legal

The Washington Consensus

State industries should be privatized
Industries should be deregulated
Property rights should be protected

The Washington Consensus:

Guiding principles:

Nobody can actually plan growth, you need to just make room for it to happen organically.
Stability is of high importance for investors.
Economic activity requires complementary policies that work together, not one panacea.
Markets know better than politicians.

Does this sound familiar?

This is Neoliberalism. This is Neoclassical Economics. Reaganism. Thatcherism.

Policy First

Like Dependency Theory, the proponents of The Washington Consensus quickly entered politics.
Groups of economists traveled to advise developing countries.
The most notorious clique, called “The Chicago Boys” were famous for not caring about the regime type of the countries they helped.

Policy First

Like Dependency Theory, the Washington Consensus was deployed before it was widely tested.
Like Dependency Theory, the Washington Consensus had ideological underpinnings that contributed to it's successes and failures.
But Unlike Dependency Theory, the proponents of the Washington Consensus never considered it a frame or a critical theory.

Washington Consensus Today

After the 2008 Financial Crisis, the consensus crumbled. Many still hold to be basic theory: Market-driven reforms are best, but it is no longer a consensus. It might not ever have been a real theory.
However, the Washington Consensus is still the “Orthodox” position for policymakers. There is no alternative paradigm yet. It is simply no longer consensus.

Washington Consensus Today

More recent approaches are focused on microfoundations:

Understanding leader preferences
Understanding regime type incentives
Understanding specific policies required by specific contexts
Emphasis on flexibility

Thinking about China

China upsets every theory of development.

Rapid, radical transition in the Deng Xiaoping period
Markets, privatization, business introduced and promoted in the 80s
Political and economic experimentation led to an explosion of ideas and progress.
People began to demand democratization.

Thinking about China

Democratic reforms were not permitted.

Thinking about China

As democracy was being crushed, people thought it was the end of market liberalization and capitalism in China (which theories might make you think this?)

Thinking about China

Instead, markets marched on. Privatization continued.
China joined the WTO. China became a trade giant, from nothing.
China is now a central pillar of the world economy, but has gotten more authoritarian with time.

Thinking about China

China did not require market-nurturing institutions like democracy, individual liberties, or even robust property rights
China did not develop or use the kinds of sophisticated institutions other places have required. Reforms appear random and authoritarian.
China has managed to avoid plundering the elites that were enriched by it's reforms
