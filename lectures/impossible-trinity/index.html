Trilemmas:

    • Monetary Independence, Exchange-Rate Flexibility, Free Capital Flows

The Policy "Trilemma"

Monetary Independence

  • Control over domestic interest rates and money supply
  • Allows tools for fighting inflation, dealing with debt, or stimulating the economy.
  • Alternative:
    • Currency Unions, Currency pegs, external control

Fixed Exchange-Rate

  • Government policy sets exchange rates (requires large reserves for buying back currency).
  • Most common method is "pegging" to another currency or gold.
  • Alternative
    • Floating Exchange Rate: Rates set by markets.

Free Capital Flows

  • No restrictions on how much money you can convert into foreign currency
  • No limits on outgoing investments
  • Alternative
    • Capital Restrictions: Limits on outgoing capital.

"Closed Financial Markets"

  • Combo:
    • Monetary policy autonomy
    • Fixed exchange rates
  • Financial Autarky
    • Preferred choice of most developing countries in the 1980s

"Floating Exchange-Rate Regime"

  • Combo:
    • Monetary independence
    • Financial integration
  • Preferred choice of the USA, UK

"Monetary Dependence"

  • Combo:
    • Exchange-rate stability (pegged currency)
    • Financial integration
  • No monetary independence
    • Preferred choice of Eurozone, Argentina in 90s, Hong Kong
