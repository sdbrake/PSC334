MNCS and Politics

Post WWI World:

  • American corporations began establishing local factories in other countries.
  • Europe and Japan started joining them in the 1960s
  • This gave rise to a new actor:
    • The NMC/TNC/MNE/TNE

Nobody knows how to think about this.

Economic production is organized globally but political systems remain organized nationally.

Contradictions:

  • They violate the law of comparative advantage.
    • Can you figure out how?
  • Some are larger in income and wealth than many countries.
    • But they do not have sovereign authority or power.
  • They use non-market means of allocating resources.
    • But we previously agreed that markets are the most efficient way to allocate resources. Are they stupid?

The specific behavior of MNCs has a name:

FDI

Effects of FDI:

  • Moves Capital
    • From where it is abundant to where it is scarce.
    • But... there is also FDI between similarly endowed nations. It's the majority.
  • Cultural Imperialism
    • Causes consumer patterns in MNC countries to be replicated in branch countries
    • Coca-Cola, sometimes war

The Question:

  • Why do MNCs exist?
    • Why acquire their competitors and supply chain, avoiding the market?
    • Why do business internationally, with all the complications, instead of just lending money?
    • Why not just do business with firms in other countries?

Answer One:

Product Cycle Theory

  • Reymond Verner came up with a theory that there is a natural cycle.
    • 1. Firm introduces a product for the domestic market.
    • 2. Firm exports the product to foreign markets.
    • 3. Firm invests in production in the foreign market to sell there.
    • 4. Firm exports the product back home.

It's a natural process with explainable steps, not a strategy.

Answer Two:

Transaction Costs

  • There are LOCATIONAL advantages that markets cannot alleviate
  • Things the MNC can use, but not contract upon or own.
  • Examples:
    • Proprietary Assets (Public goods, ingangibles, or diffuse goods)
    • Relationships (especially in the service industry)
    • Natural Resource investments
    • Market oriented investments (getting market access to restricted markets)
    • Efficiency oriented investments

It's about leveraging local advantages.

Answer Three:

Market Failures

  • Horizontal Integration:
    • A firm creates multiple production facilities that produce the same good. (buying or founding what would normally be competitors. Toyota making cars in Japan and in the US)
    • Market failure: Intangible assets can’t be sold
  • Vertical Integration:
    • A firm buys the production of intermediate goods.
    • Market failure: Specific Asset: Some of these things require long term relationships that contracts can’t provide, like a supply chain.

The entire process is called: Global Value Chain:

  • MNC advantages:
    • Bringing resources
    • Bringing technology/expertise
    • More positive externalities
    • Access to marketing networks
    • Raising wages
  • MNC disadvantages:
    • Crowding out domestic investment
    • MNCs extracting fees
    • Requiring purchases from corporation
    • Drive domestic firms out of business
    • Less power for domestic government to control economy

FDI and Democracy

  • Democracy encourages FDI
    • Property Rights
    • Lower Risk
  • Democracy discourages FDI
    • Local interests demand protection
    • Collective action problems

Reseach supports this: Democracies with weak property rights have less FDI
