the background

The Corn Laws

“Corn” means wheat here.
Britain had corn growers, but not enough to feed the whole island. They wanted food security.
Enacted to protect the wheat market from price shocks caused by the Napoleonic Wars.
It allowed free entry of wheat when the price was above a threshold, and prevented it when the price was below a threshold.
“

“to prevent grain from being at any time, either so dear that the poor cannot subsist, or so cheap that the farmer cannot live by growing of it.”

part two

The Debate

the debate

Bread Prices vs Wages

argument one

Bread Prices vs Wages

Protectionist Arguments

  • The industrialists thought they could lower wages through repeal: If bread prices were lower, wages would go down and workers could be paid less
  • Foreign monopolies would outsell the local food market, raising prices of food in the long run
  • Agricultural decline would force more workers into industry, lowering industrial wages anyway

Free Trader Arguments

  • Free Traders argued that repeal would allow for specialization
  • Specialization would increase trade revenue could increase workers’ wages

the debate

Class Conflict

argument two

Class Conflict

Protectionist Argument

  • Free Traders are trying to enrich the industrialists at the expense of the workers!

Free Trader Argument

  • Protectionists are trying to enrich the aristocracy at the expense of the farm laborers!

the debate

The Taxation of Landowners

argument three

The Taxation of Landowners

Protectionist Argument

  • Landowners paid disproportionate taxes, they should be protected.

Free Trader Argument

  • Land is more valuable than ever, Landowners should actually be taxed more!

the debate

Who Exploits Whom?

argument four

Who Exploits Whom?

Protectionist Argument

  • Capitalists take more of the profit from industrial laborers than landowners take from farm laborers.
  • Take away their income to empower poor workers

Free Trader Argument

  • Landowners make rents off of poor farmers – protecting trade doesn’t actually help the laborers
  • Take away their income to empower poor farmers

the debate

National Importance of Agriculture vs Manufacturing

argument five

The National Importance of Agriculture vs Manufacturing

Protectionist Argument

  • Agriculture produces food, which is critical to the nation
  • Reliance on foreign food would threaten security
  • Agriculture employs more of the work force
  • Land is the basis of power and wealth

Free Trader Argument

  • Agriculture is just a business like any other
  • Industry is connected to progress and growth: we should empower it

the debate

The National Interest

argument six

The National Interest

Protectionist Argument

  • Other countries will take advantage of Britain lowering tariffs
  • Self-sufficiency is a matter of national interest
  • The government depends on tariff revenues

Free Trader Argument

  • Other nations will probably lower tariffs if Britain does
  • Manufacturing exports could destroy other countries’ manufacturing sectors
  • Exporters will make so much money that it will make up for it in taxes

Issue Linkage

issue linkage

The Corn Laws reveal a trend

Agriculture causes lots of trade disputes
Countries hang on to agricultural tariffs longer than other types
Farm lobbies usually form influential interest groups.

issue linkage

When problems that affect two different sectors are connected via institutions or negotiation strategy.

Cross-Sector Interests

Those who demand protection usually have an advantage
Collective Action Problem
Institutional bias
Status quo bias
Smaller groups can combine to counter farmers through linkage

Institutions and Credibility

Institutions allow multiple issues to be negotiated at once
Institutions provide credible long-term linkage

issue linkage

Evidence

Over the history of GATT and WTO negotiation rounds, liberalization was more successful when joint negotiation in multiple sectors happened at once
Institutions with narrow purposes and low levels of institutionalization produced much less liberalization of trade

How institutions link issues in trade
