Three Schools of IPE

IPE Theory

Two Big Questions

How are decisions about how to use resources made?
What are the consequences of these decisions?

approach one

Economic Nationalism

The Classic:  ·  Economic Nationalism

Mercantilism

Markets are fixed
Power and Wealth are directly connected
Trade is predatory
Get the gold!

The Evolution:  ·  Economic Nationalism

Economic Nationalism

States compete for economic power
Exports better than Imports
Zero sum game

The First Question:  ·  Economic Nationalism

How are decisions about how to use resources made?

States guide resource allocations.
States seek their own power and wealth.
States try to gain the upper hand over each other in trade.

approach two

Economic Liberalism

The Classic  ·  Economic Liberalism

Economic Liberalism

Economics is complex
Markets are most efficient
Competition is good
Power and Wealth can and should be completely separate
Positive sum game

The Evolution  ·  Economic Liberalism

Neoliberalism

Fiscal Discipline
Insulated Monetary Policy
Private sector better than government
Protect property rights
The Washington Consensus

The First Question:  ·  Economic Liberalism

How are decisions about how to use resources made?

Market-based transactions guide all good decisions.
State action that limits the free market is almost always bad.
Self-interested firms tend to make the best decisions.

approach three

Marxism

The Classic  ·  Marxism

Classical Marxism

There exists a state called “capitalism”
Private ownership of capital
Wage labor
Capitalism is doomed to collapse because:
Competition concentrates capital
Profits decrease over time
Productivity and wages decrease with time

The Evolution  ·  Dependency theory

Dependency Theory

Capitalism exists among nations as well.
Powerful countries drain resources from poor countries:
Colonization
Investment and Trade

The First Question:  ·  Marxism

How are decisions about how to use resources made?

Firms, controlled by the capitalist class, set policy.
Class conflict drives all major decisions.
Exploitation of the working class drives economic and political reality.

applying the second question:

What are the consequences of the choices we make?

Economic Nationalism: Will our choices bring more wealth home or send it overseas?
Economic Liberalism: Will our choices enable governments to have credible agreements that can lead to more, freer trade?
Marxism: How much can the system take before The Revolution?

applying the implicit second question:

What kinds of changes should we look for?

Economic Nationalism: Is there some alternative for allocation of resources that would enhance the nation’s power in the international system?
Economic Liberalism: Is there some alternative allocation of resources that would enable the society to improve its standard of living?
Marxism: Is there an alternative political and economic system that will promote a more equitable distribution of income?
